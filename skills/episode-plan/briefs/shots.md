# One chapter's shots

The chapter's beats are written and checked, and they are in your brief. You write its shots
from them: one line each of picture and script. A shot is the smallest thing a builder is handed,
so it says exactly what is seen and heard and for how long. The words are already written: each
beat carries the passage the voice says, and you decide where the picture cuts under it.

## Read

- The beats in your brief. They are your whole source: do not change them, and do not write a
  shot that shows or says something no beat does.
- The plan's top layer, as your brief lists it: the spine, the chapter's row, the voice and the
  music.
- [../../motion-direction/references/techniques.md](../../motion-direction/references/techniques.md)
  for moves that have names.
- [../references/sections.md](../references/sections.md) for the form of what you write.

## From a beat to its shots

One to three shots a beat, numbered under it (2A.1, 2A.2), adding up to the beat's seconds
exactly.

- The shots perform the beat's `change`, in order. Each shot does one thing: one `move`.
- The first shot starts on the beat's `enters`; the last ends on its `result` and leaves its
  `leaves` in place for the next beat.
- The voice is the beat's `says`, cut into the shots' lines in order. Put together, a beat's
  lines are its passage, word for word: you add no word, drop none and change none. Cut at the
  end of a sentence, or at a clause where the picture changes; a sentence may run across two
  shots when the picture builds with it.
- A beat whose `says` is empty plays in silence: its shots have no `vo`.
- Time each shot to its line: the picture changes on the word that names the change.
- The words on screen name what the beat's `demands` point at. They never repeat the voice word
  for word.

## A shot

- `move`: one named move. Use a technique's name where one fits; otherwise a plain verb for what
  happens: split, look up, sort, transform, morph, merge, connect, trace a path, compare.
- `shows`: what is on screen and what it does, concretely, in a sentence or two, in words: no
  positions or sizes in pixels, no fonts, no colour values.
- `vo`: the shot's piece of its beat's passage, word for word. Leave it out for a shot that
  plays in silence.
- `on`: the words on screen, word for word, six or fewer as a rule. `at` is when they land, in
  seconds from the shot's start. Where the voice says a figure in words, the screen carries it
  in digits: the exact number is the picture's. Leave the picture's two standing lines out of it (the source,
  and the brief's `label` for made-up material): they are part of the look, not of this shot's
  words, and no other word for "made up" goes on screen.
- `sound`: the one sound that marks the move, or nothing.

## The numbers a shot must pass

These are checked the moment you report, and a shot that fails comes back to you:

- The voice reads about 2.2 words a second, and a line ends at least half a second before its
  shot does. A 20-word line needs a shot of 9.6 seconds or more.
- A shot carries about 19 spoken words, never more than 26. A longer line is two shots.
- Words on screen hold for 0.3 seconds a word plus half a second, counted from when they land.
- The shots of a beat add up to the beat's seconds.
- A beat's lines add up to its passage, word for word.

Make the numbers work by moving the cut or the seconds between a beat's shots, never by
changing a word. A passage that cannot fit its beat however it is cut is the beat's to fix: say
so in your report.

## Report

The chapter's shots in a line, and anything a beat did not say that you needed. Then your file
as JSON, whole.
